# VCPeer Book — Competitive & Growth Strategy (2026-10-23)

Scope note: this file covers competitive positioning, user research, and
go-to-market — not page/API/feature build-out. Check for a sibling
`PLAN-2026-10-23-DEEP.md` (pages/APIs/features) before treating this as the
only planning doc for the day; as of this writing that file does not exist
in this repo, so nothing here duplicates it.

Current reality check (from repo + live box, not assumed):
- VCPeer Book is self-hosted Cal.com (`cal.diy`, MIT) rebranded, live at
  `https://book.vcpeer.com`, backed by Postgres + the stock Cal.com image
  (`calcom/cal.com:v6.2.0-arm`) — no Cal.com source forked.
- `NEXT_PUBLIC_DISABLE_SIGNUP=true` in production today — per `.env`'s own
  comment it is currently run as "an internal team booking tool, not a
  public multi-tenant SaaS." Only one real org/admin user exists
  (`subasiarhan3@gmail.com`), with zero outside tenants.
  **This means VCPeer Book today has zero self-serve signups and zero
  paying customers** — it is a booking page for VCPeer's own
  demos/support calls, not yet a product other businesses can sign up for.
- Each booking mints a unique, per-booking VCPeer video room
  (`https://vcpeer.com/join/<uuid>` via the built-in Jitsi app) — not a
  shared static link, not Zoom/Google Meet.
- Sibling product `vcpeer` (the video-call app) is positioned and shipped
  as "Free P2P Video Calls, No Signup, No Time Limit" — fully free, no
  account required, browser-based, OSS.
- Known unresolved gaps (from `NOTES.md`): no real Google/Office 365
  calendar connected for conflict-checking yet (only internal VCPeer-Book
  bookings block slots), brand-color drift pending an admin login, DKIM
  unconfirmed. These are real limitations to factor into persona/journey
  below, not hidden.

## 1. Direct competitors

- **Calendly** — market leader, scheduling-link category creator.
  - Pricing (2026): Free (1 event type, 1 calendar, 1-on-1 only) → Standard
    $10/seat/mo annual ($12 monthly) → Teams $16/seat/mo annual ($20
    monthly, round-robin/routing) → Enterprise ~$15k/yr for ~50 seats. AI
    Notetaker add-on +$8/seat/yr.
  - Strongest feature: distribution — deepest integration bench (Zoom,
    Google/Outlook/Salesforce/HubSpot, Zapier, native routing forms),
    biggest brand recognition, so bookers rarely hesitate on trust.
  - Weakest point: free tier is deliberately crippled (one event type, one
    calendar) to force upgrade; every team member needs a paid seat even if
    they book twice a month; video is just a Zoom/Meet link, not a bundled
    product — you still pay Zoom separately for anything beyond 40 minutes
    group calls.

- **Cal.com (hosted Cloud)** — open-source scheduling, the direct upstream
  of what VCPeer Book runs self-hosted.
  - Pricing (2026): Free/Individuals (1 user, unlimited event types,
    Stripe/PayPal payments, Cal Video) → Teams ~$12/user/mo annual
    (~$15 monthly: round-robin, collective availability, team analytics) →
    Organizations ~$28/user/mo annual (~$37 monthly: SSO/SAML, subdomain,
    unlimited sub-teams) → Enterprise custom.
  - Strongest feature: the free tier is genuinely usable (unlimited event
    types for 1 user, no artificial 1-event-type cap like Calendly) and
    it's the same open-source codebase VCPeer Book already runs, so
    feature parity is near-total by construction.
  - Weakest point: self-hosting it yourself (the free path to avoid seat
    fees) requires exactly the ops work this repo's own `RUNBOOK.md`
    documents — ARM image pinning, Postgres backups, cron sidecars, branding
    patches via `replace-placeholder.sh`. Cal.com Cloud's AI/video minutes
    are metered ($0.29/min over included credits) — not simply free.

- **SavvyCal** — scheduling-UX challenger, known for the calendar-overlay
  booking experience (invitee sees the host's actual availability
  overlaid on their own calendar instead of a flat slot grid).
  - Pricing (2026): no standing free plan for active use (only a trial +
    30-day refund guarantee) → Basic $10/user/mo annual ($12 monthly:
    unlimited links, round-robin, workflows) → Premium $16/user/mo annual
    ($20 monthly: Stripe payments, custom domains, branding removal) →
    Pro $32/user/mo annual ($40 monthly: white-label, advanced workflows).
  - Strongest feature: the calendar-overlay picker itself — measurably
    higher booking-completion rates in their own case studies because the
    invitee isn't mentally cross-referencing two calendars.
  - Weakest point: no free-forever tier at all, so solo users who just want
    one link with no card on file have nowhere to land; pricing climbs
    fastest of the four for branding removal + custom domain (gated behind
    the $16+ tier, same as Calendly).

- **Acuity Scheduling (Squarespace)** — service-business scheduling
  (salons, clinics, coaches), not a developer/SaaS-team tool.
  - Pricing (2026): no permanent free plan (7-day trial only) → Starter
    $16/mo annual ($20 monthly, 1 calendar) → Standard $27/mo annual
    ($34 monthly, up to 6 calendars, SMS reminders, packages/memberships) →
    Premium $49/mo annual ($61 monthly, HIPAA BAA, multi-location) +
    Stripe/Square/PayPal fees (2.9%+$0.30) on top of every paid booking.
  - Strongest feature: built-in commerce for service businesses — package
    bundles, gift certificates, membership billing, group classes — none
    of the other four touch this.
  - Weakest point: flat per-account pricing with no free tier at all
    penalizes a true solo user the same as a 6-location business; video
    conferencing isn't a first-class feature (it's an Zoom-link add-on at
    best), so it competes on commerce, not on bundled calls.

## 2. Exact differentiation plan (beat each on a named axis)

- **vs. Calendly — price axis, concretely:** Calendly forces a paid seat
  the moment a second event type or a second connected calendar is needed
  ($10-20/seat/mo). VCPeer Book's underlying stack (Cal.com free/Individual
  tier equivalent, self-hosted) already runs **unlimited event types on one
  shared Oracle box for $0 marginal seat cost** — the real cost is the
  box's own hosting, already sunk for the rest of the VCPeer suite. The
  concrete move: once self-serve signup is turned on (see Section 5), price
  every VCPeer Book seat at **$0** for solo/small-team use and only charge
  for the thing Calendly charges for last (custom domain + branding
  removal), matching SavvyCal/Calendly's gated tier but starting from a
  genuinely free full-feature base instead of a crippled one.

- **vs. Calendly/SavvyCal/Acuity — bundled video axis, concretely:** all
  three competitors treat video as "paste a Zoom/Meet link" or a metered
  add-on. VCPeer Book already mints a **unique, per-booking VCPeer video
  room** (`vcpeer.com/join/<uuid>`) automatically via the Jitsi app, and the
  underlying `vcpeer` video product is explicitly "no signup, no time
  limit" today. The concrete move: market this literally as "your booking
  link already includes unlimited-length video calls — no Zoom account,
  no per-minute AI-notetaker credit metering (Calendly charges $0.29/min
  equivalent via its add-on; Cal.com Cloud meters Cal Video AI the same
  way)." No other competitor in Section 1 can say "the video is free and
  unmetered" truthfully today — confirmed from each vendor's own 2026
  pricing pages above.

- **vs. Cal.com Cloud — self-host/ownership axis, concretely:** Cal.com
  Cloud's Organizations tier ($28-37/user/mo) is the only path to a
  company subdomain + full data control on their hosted product. VCPeer
  Book already has a branded subdomain (`book.vcpeer.com`) and full
  Postgres-level data ownership (nightly backup cron already live,
  `/srv/backups/vcpeer-book/`, 14-day retention) **at $0/seat**, because
  it's the identical open-source codebase self-hosted. The concrete move:
  publish the actual total cost of self-hosting (one shared Oracle Ampere
  box amortized across the whole VCPeer suite — not a dedicated server per
  customer) next to Cal.com Cloud's $28-37/seat Organizations price, with
  the real `RUNBOOK.md` steps linked as proof it isn't vaporware.

- **vs. SavvyCal — workflow-they-get-wrong axis, concretely:** SavvyCal has
  zero free-forever tier (confirmed above — trial + refund only), which
  means a referred solo contact has nowhere to land without a card. VCPeer
  Book's planned public tier (Section 5) keeps a true always-free solo
  plan (matching Cal.com's Individuals tier, not Calendly's crippled one),
  closing exactly the gap SavvyCal leaves open for price-sensitive solo
  referrals.

- **vs. Acuity — commerce axis, concretely:** Acuity's commerce features
  (packages, memberships, gift certificates) are out of scope for VCPeer
  Book's current B2B/dev-tool persona (Section 3) — not a fight worth
  picking. Differentiation here is to **not** compete on service-business
  commerce and instead stay anchored to the developer/small-team booking +
  free video niche where Acuity has no video story at all.

## 3. Primary user persona / ICP

- **Who exactly:** a founder, freelance developer, or small (2-10 person)
  software/agency team already living in the rest of the VCPeer-adjacent
  stack (Supabase/Neon-style tools, Resend-style email, self-hosted OSS
  preference) — the same ICP already named in memory for Scribase/VCPeer
  generally. Not enterprise sales teams (no SSO/SCIM story), not service
  businesses needing commerce (Acuity's turf).
- **Job to be done:** "let a prospect, user, or support contact pick a slot
  on my calendar and land on a working video call, without me paying a
  per-seat fee for a tool I use maybe twice a week, and without sending
  them to a Zoom/Google Meet link I don't control."
- **Trigger moment:** sending a "book a time with me" link in a cold email,
  a GitHub README, a support reply, or a landing-page CTA — the exact
  moment Calendly's free tier (1 event type) or SavvyCal's no-free-tier
  wall would normally force a credit card.
- **What they already have today that VCPeer Book must slot into:** a
  Google/Outlook calendar they expect real conflict-checking against (the
  `NOTES.md` gap — zero calendar credentials connected in production today
  — is the single biggest thing standing between this persona and trusting
  the tool with real external meetings).

## 4. User journey map (discovery → paying/retained)

1. **Discovery** — sees a `book.vcpeer.com/<handle>/<slug>` link shared by
   someone else (cold email, README, support thread) or finds VCPeer Book
   via the main `vcpeer.com` video-call product's own audience (existing
   "free, no signup" video users who need a booking layer on top).
2. **First booking page view** — loads the public slot picker; sees VCPeer
   branding (logo, `#4678F9` accent — pending the in-app fix in `NOTES.md`),
   confirms a time.
3. **Confirmation** — reads the Terms/Privacy links (now pointing at
   `vcpeer.com/privacy`, per `NOTES.md`'s shipped fix) and submits.
4. **Email confirmation received** — via `bookings@vcpeer.com`
   (Scribase Mail SMTP) with the unique `vcpeer.com/join/<uuid>` video link.
5. **The call itself** — joins the Jitsi-powered VCPeer room at the
   scheduled time; this is the actual value-delivery moment (a working,
   unmetered video call with no Zoom account needed).
6. **(Gap today, not yet reachable)** Host wants to sign up for their own
   VCPeer Book account to send their own booking links — **blocked**,
   because `NEXT_PUBLIC_DISABLE_SIGNUP=true` in production right now. This
   is the step that must open before any of this persona can become a
   second user, let alone a paying one.
7. **(Future) Self-serve signup** — host creates an account, connects a
   real Google/Outlook calendar (today: not possible, zero OAuth client
   configured — see `NOTES.md` item 4), sets availability, publishes their
   own link.
8. **(Future) Upgrade trigger** — host wants a custom domain or branding
   removal (the one axis VCPeer Book can credibly charge for, see Section
   2) → becomes a paying/retained user.

- **Biggest drop-off risk step: step 6 → 7.** Today there is no way for a
  second human being to become a VCPeer Book user at all — signup is
  hard-disabled and real calendar-conflict checking doesn't exist yet. Every
  booker who has a great experience at steps 2-5 currently has zero path to
  becoming a user themselves; 100% of that word-of-mouth potential is lost
  at the product-gate level, not a marketing problem. This is the single
  highest-leverage fix available and it is a config + OAuth-client change,
  not a redesign.

## 5. Three concrete growth moves (specific to this product)

1. **Turn on self-serve signup with a real free tier, gated by the one
   thing that's actually hard to copy.** Flip `NEXT_PUBLIC_DISABLE_SIGNUP`
   off for a new multi-tenant path (keep the current internal admin account
   exactly as-is on its own org), and price it: free forever for unlimited
   event types + the bundled unmetered VCPeer video room (matching Cal.com
   Individuals, beating Calendly's 1-event-type free cap), with a paid tier
   reserved only for custom domain + branding removal. This directly closes
   the Section-4 drop-off and is the precondition for every other growth
   move below — without it there is no second user to grow.

2. **Ship real calendar-conflict checking before any public push**, by
   creating the Google Cloud OAuth client named as a gap in `NOTES.md`
   (console project, Calendar API, redirect URI
   `https://book.vcpeer.com/api/integrations/googlecalendar/callback`) and
   wiring `GOOGLE_API_CREDENTIALS` into `/etc/products/vcpeer-book.env`.
   This is the credibility gate for the exact ICP in Section 3 (developers
   who already distrust tools that double-book them) — launching self-serve
   signup (move 1) without this risks the first wave of new users getting
   double-booked and churning before a second booking ever happens.

3. **Cross-sell from the existing `vcpeer` video-call audience, not cold
   acquisition.** `vcpeer.com`'s own landing page already markets "Free
   P2P Video Calls, No Signup, No Time Limit" to founders/developers/small
   teams who are the identical ICP as Section 3. Add a single, specific CTA
   on a completed VCPeer video call ("Turn this into a recurring booking
   link — book.vcpeer.com") pointed at the new signup flow from move 1.
   This reuses traffic VCPeer already owns instead of buying new traffic,
   and is the most direct path to a first real external paying/retained
   VCPeer Book user, as opposed to generic SEO/ads work with no connection
   to an existing audience.
